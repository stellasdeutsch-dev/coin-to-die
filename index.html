The Dirichlet model: from a coin to a K-sided die

Lecture 3 learned one probability (hit or miss). Today we learn a whole list of them: which role the enemy picks first, which word comes in all-chat. Same idea, same one-line update. Nothing assumed; every tool is explained here.

Part 1 · The question

20 matches against one captain. What will they first-pick?

You keep playing against the same enemy captain. You write down which role they first-pick. There are K = 5 possible roles. After 20 matches:

C9carry9/20 = 0.45
M6mid6/20 = 0.30
O3offlane3/20 = 0.15
S2soft support2/20 = 0.10
H0hard support0/20 = 0 ?!

The simple estimate (the MLE, “count ÷ total”) is shown under each role. It says the captain will never first-pick a hard support. Is that believable? After only 20 games, no. It just hasn't happened yet. This is the zero-count problem again, and it gets worse the more categories you have: with 120 heroes instead of 5 roles, most heroes would have count 0.

In Lecture 3 we fixed this for a coin (2 outcomes) with a Beta prior. Today we do it for a die with K outcomes. The prior is called the Dirichlet (say “dee-ree-KLAY”).

Two outcomes vs K outcomes
Lecture 3Today
datahit / missone of K roles
unknownone θθ₁, …, θK
likelihoodBinomialMultinomial
priorBeta(a, b)Dir(α₁, …, αK)
updatea + hits, b + missesαk + countk

Remember it: Dirichlet is to a K-sided die what Beta is to a coin.

Part 2 · Toolbox

Five tools, each with a tiny example

Tool 1 · A probability vector

θ = (θ₁, …, θK): one probability per outcome, adding to 1

θ₁ + θ₂ + … + θK = 1, every θk ≥ 0

For the captain: θ = (θcarry, θmid, θoff, θsoft, θhard), for example (0.4, 0.3, 0.15, 0.1, 0.05). The set of all such vectors is called the probability simplex. One draw from θ is a Categorical outcome.

Try it: θcarry = 0.4, θmid = 0.3, θoff = 0.2, θsoft = 0.05. What is θhard?

1 − 0.95 = 0.05.

Tool 2 · The likelihood is a product of powers

Each match multiplies in its role's θ

p(D | θ) = θ₁N₁ · θ₂N₂ · … · θKNK = Πk θkNk

Nk is how often role k happened. Π means “multiply over all k”. Only the counts matter, not the order of the matches. The counts are called sufficient statistics: all you need to know about the data.

Try it: write p(D | θ) for carry, mid, carry.

θcarry² · θmid.

Tool 3 · Constants don't matter (∝)

The multinomial coefficient drops out

Mult(N₁, …, NK | θ) = N! / (N₁! ⋯ NK!) · Π θkNk

If you only know the counts, the probability gets a counting factor N!/(N₁!⋯NK!): the number of orders (like C(n, k) for a coin). It has no θ in it, so for the posterior we drop it and write ∝.

Tool 4 · The Dirichlet distribution

A belief about a whole probability vector

Dir(θ | α) = (1/B(α)) · Πk θkαk − 1

α = (α₁, …, αK) are positive numbers, the pseudo-counts: “as if I had already seen αk − 1 picks of role k”. B(α) only makes the total 1. With K = 2 it is exactly the Beta distribution.

Try it: what does Dir(1, 1, 1, 1, 1) believe?

Every probability vector is equally believable: the flat “no idea” prior, like Beta(1, 1).

Tool 5 · Three numbers to read off a Dirichlet

α₀, the mean and the strength

α₀ = α₁ + … + αK,    E[θk] = αk / α₀

α₀ is the strength of the belief (the equivalent sample size). Dir(2, 2, 2, 2, 2) and Dir(20, 20, 20, 20, 20) both have mean 1/5 for every role, but the second is worth 100 pseudo-matches and is much more certain. Same rule as Lecture 3: bigger total, narrower belief.

Remember it: θ is a list of probabilities that add to 1; the likelihood is Π θkNk; the Dirichlet Π θkαk−1 is a belief about that list; mean αk/α₀.

Part 4 · The derivation

Prior Dir(α) + counts N₁, …, NK

Bayesp(θ | D) ∝ p(D | θ) · p(θ)posterior ∝ likelihood × prior; the bottom line p(D) has no θ
plug in∝ Πk θkNk · Πk θkαk − 1Tool 2 times Tool 4; constants dropped (Tool 3)
same base= Πk θkNk θkαk − 1put the two θk of each role next to each other
add powers= Πk θkαk + Nk − 1θaθb = θa+b
read offθ | D ~ Dir(α₁ + N₁, …, αK + NK)a Dirichlet shape with new exponents (+1)

In words: add each role's count to its α. That's the whole update, exactly like hits → a and misses → b for the coin.

Conjugate: Dirichlet prior in, Dirichlet posterior out. That's why the Dirichlet is the standard prior for categorical data.

Our captain, flat prior Dir(1, 1, 1, 1, 1)
Dir(1+9, 1+6, 1+3, 1+2, 1+0) = Dir(10, 7, 4, 3, 1)

α₀ after the update = 25 = 5 (prior) + 20 (matches).

Remember it: Dirichlet prior × multinomial likelihood = Dirichlet posterior: αk + Nk.

Part 6 · Three numbers from the posterior

Mean, MAP, MLE for each role

Write the posterior as Dir(α′) with α′k = αk + Nk and α′₀ = α₀ + N. Then, for each role k:

Posterior mean(αk + Nk) / (α₀ + N)

The balance point. It is also the predictive: the chance that the next pick is role k.

MAP (peak)(Nk + αk − 1) / (N + α₀ − K)

The single most believable vector. Like the MLE with αk − 1 pseudo-counts added.

MLENk / N

Data only. With a flat prior (all αk = 1) the MAP equals the MLE.

rolecountMLE Nk/Nmean, α = 1mean, α = 2MAP, α = 2
carry90.4510/25 = 0.4011/30 ≈ 0.3710/25 = 0.40
mid60.307/25 = 0.288/30 ≈ 0.277/25 = 0.28
offlane30.154/25 = 0.165/30 ≈ 0.174/25 = 0.16
soft support20.103/25 = 0.124/30 ≈ 0.133/25 = 0.12
hard support00 ✗1/25 = 0.042/30 ≈ 0.071/25 = 0.04

Where does the MAP formula come from? You maximise Σ (Nk + αk − 1) log θk, but the θ's must add up to 1, so you can't move each one freely. The slides handle this with a Lagrange multiplier λ, a trick for “maximise under a constraint”. The result is θk ∝ Nk + αk − 1, then you divide by the total so everything adds to 1. That total is N + α₀ − K.

Why is the predictive the posterior mean? Lump all other roles into “not k”. Then θk behaves like a coin's probability, with a Beta(α′k, α′₀ − α′k) belief. The chance of “k” next is that Beta's mean, α′k/α′₀. Exactly Lecture 3's rule.

Remember it: predictive = (αk + Nk)/(α₀ + N). With αk = 1 this is add-one smoothing: no outcome is ever impossible.

Part 7 · The exam task (2025 Final, Task 6(a))

Bag of words in Dota all-chat

The task: predict the next word based only on how often each word appeared. That is the bag-of-words model: every word is an independent draw from a Categorical(θ) over the vocabulary; the order of words is ignored.

Vocabulary (K = 5): gg, wp, ez, ?, glhf. Chat seen: “gg wp gg ez gg ? gg wp”. Counts: gg 4, wp 2, ez 1, ? 1, glhf 0. N = 8.

Exam answer in three lines: model Categorical / multinomial; prior Dir(α); posterior Dir(α + N). With a flat prior:

posteriorDir(1+4, 1+2, 1+1, 1+1, 1+0) = Dir(5, 3, 2, 2, 1)add counts to α
totalα′₀ = 5 + 8 = 13prior strength + words seen
P(gg next)5/13 ≈ 0.38MLE would say 4/8 = 0.5
P(glhf next)1/13 ≈ 0.08MLE would say 0: impossible
The follow-up questions on the same exam

2025 Task 6(b) asked: if the next word should depend on the last word, what model is that? A Markov chain (Day 15). And the 2026 true/false: “bag of words is a good model to predict the next word in a stream of text” is false: it ignores order.

Remember it: bag of words = Categorical words, Dirichlet prior, posterior α + counts, predictive (αk + Nk)/(α₀ + N).

Part 8 · Probability of counts (Week 3, Question 3)

When θ is known: the multinomial

Sometimes the task gives you θ and asks for the probability of a set of counts. Suppose the captain's first picks are carry 0.3, mid 0.3, offlane 0.2, support 0.2. What is the chance of exactly 4 carries, 3 mids, 1 offlane and 2 supports in 10 matches?

P = N! / (N₁! N₂! ⋯ NK!) · θ₁N₁ ⋯ θKNK

The fraction counts how many orders give these counts; the product is the chance of one such order.

orders10!/(4! 3! 1! 2!) = 12 6003 628 800 / (24 · 6 · 1 · 2)
one order0.3⁴ · 0.3³ · 0.2¹ · 0.2² = 0.3⁷ · 0.2³powers of the same number add up
result12 600 · 0.0002187 · 0.008 ≈ 0.022about 2%
Part 9 · Conjugate priors recap

One pattern for every model

A prior is conjugate to a likelihood if the posterior is in the same family as the prior. Then updating is just “prior numbers + data summaries”. Week 4's “one formula to rule them all” says exactly this for the whole exponential family: new hyperparameters = old ones + the sufficient statistics of the data.

data (likelihood)conjugate priorposteriorDota example
Bernoulli / Binomial: k hits in nBeta(a, b)Beta(a + k, b + n − k)Mirana arrows
Geometric: losses kᵢ before a winBeta(a, b)Beta(a + n, b + Σkᵢ)games before a win
Categorical / Multinomial: counts NkDir(α)Dir(α + N)first-pick role, chat words
Exponential: waits xᵢGamma(a, b)Gamma(a + n, b + Σxᵢ)minutes between ganks
Poisson: counts xᵢGamma(a, b)Gamma(a + Σxᵢ, b + n)kills per game
Gaussian mean (σ² known)GaussianGaussian (Day 8)last hits

Worked Gamma example (Week 4, Question 1). Minutes between enemy ganks follow Exponential(λ). The 2026 formula sheet gives the Gamma density f(λ | a, b) = bᵃ Γ(a)⁻¹ λa−1 e−λb and its mean a/b.

Prior Gamma(2, 8): mean 2/8 = 0.25 ganks per minute. Data: 2, 5, 3, 6 minutes (n = 4, Σx = 16).

likelihoodλn e−λΣxᵢproduct of λe−λxᵢ
× priorλa−1 e−bλ · λn e−λΣxᵢ = λa+n−1 e−(b+Σxᵢ)λadd powers of λ and exponents of e
read offGamma(a + n, b + Σxᵢ) = Gamma(6, 24)mean 6/24 = 0.25

Remember it: conjugate = same family before and after. Posterior numbers = prior numbers + data summaries (counts, sums, n).

Part 10 · Learn it by heart

Six traps and five questions

Trap 1 · α₀ vs NThe predictive divides by α₀ + N (prior total + data total), not by N.
Trap 2 · MAP has −KMAP: (Nk + αk − 1)/(N + α₀ − K). The −1 is per role, so the bottom loses K.
Trap 3 · Order is ignoredBag of words throws away word order. For “next word after this word” use a Markov chain.
Trap 4 · ConstantsN!/(N₁!⋯NK!) matters for the probability of counts, but not for the posterior shape.
Trap 5 · K = 2A Dirichlet with two components is a Beta. Don't learn two theories.
Trap 6 · Flat priorDir(1, …, 1): MAP = MLE, but the predictive is still smoothed: (Nk + 1)/(N + K).

Explain it back. Answer out loud first.

What is a Dirichlet distribution, in one sentence?

A belief about a whole list of probabilities that add to 1, shaped like Π θkαk−1, where αk act as pseudo-counts.

Why do the counts get added to α?

Likelihood Π θkNk times prior Π θkαk−1: powers of the same θk add, giving θkαk+Nk−1, a Dirichlet with αk + Nk.

How does the Dirichlet fix the zero-count problem?

The predictive is (αk + Nk)/(α₀ + N). Even with Nk = 0, αk > 0 keeps it above zero: hard support gets 1/25, not 0.

What is the link between Dirichlet and Beta?

Beta is the Dirichlet with K = 2. And any single component θj of a Dirichlet follows Beta(αj, α₀ − αj).

What does “conjugate prior” mean, and name three pairs.

Same family before and after the update. Beta–Binomial, Dirichlet–Multinomial, Gamma–Exponential (or Gamma–Poisson).

Mid lane results

Your mid lane ends in win, draw or loss. Prior Dir(1, 1, 1). Over 10 games: 5 wins, 3 draws, 2 losses. Give the posterior and the predictive for each outcome.

02Easy

Reading a Dirichlet

(a) What is Dir(a, b) with K = 2? (b) Compare Dir(1, 1, 1) and Dir(10, 10, 10): mean and certainty. (c) Mean of θ₁ under Dir(6, 3, 1)?

Hint

Mean αk/α₀; α₀ is the strength.

Solution

  • (b) Both have mean 1/3 for each part; Dir(10, 10, 10) is worth 30 pseudo-observations and is much more certain.

Power runes at 6:00

You track 4 rune types (DD, haste, illusion, regen) over 12 games: 5, 4, 3, 0. (a) MLE for regen? (b) With Dir(1, 1, 1, 1), the chance the next rune is regen?

(Nk + 1)/(N + K).

MAP versus mean

All-chat bag of words

Vocabulary {gg, wp, ez, ?, glhf}. Chat: “gg ez gg wp gg ?”. State the model, the prior and the posterior, and give P(next word = gg) and P(next word = glhf) with a flat prior.

Gamma prior for gank rate

Waits between ganks ~ Exponential(λ), prior λ ~ Gamma(a, b) with density ∝ λa−1e−bλ. (a) Derive the posterior for waits x₁, …, xₙ. (b) a = 3, b = 10, waits 4, 6, 5, 5: posterior and its mean.

Hint

Likelihood λⁿ e−λΣx. Gamma mean a/b.
